Empiricism is the philosophical view that all knowledge comes from sensory experience. Here’s the gist:

1. **Sensory Experience is Key**: Empiricists argue that we can only know what we can see, hear, touch, taste, or smell. Our minds aren’t born with innate ideas; they build knowledge from what we experience in the world.
    
2. **Tabula Rasa**: This idea, often associated with John Locke, suggests that the mind is a "blank slate" at birth, and all knowledge is written on it through experience.
    
3. **Ideas and Impressions**: Empiricists distinguish between "impressions" (vivid, direct sensory experiences) and "ideas" (less intense, indirect mental copies of impressions). The ideas we form are based on the impressions we have.
    
4. **No A Priori Knowledge**: Empiricists reject the idea that we can know things independently of experience. A priori knowledge (knowledge independent of experience, like mathematical truths) is seen as irrelevant or even impossible.
    
5. **Scientific Method**: Empiricism heavily influenced the development of the scientific method, which relies on observation, experimentation, and experience to build knowledge.
    
6. **Limitations**: While empiricism emphasizes the importance of experience, critics argue that it can't account for things like abstract concepts (e.g., justice, beauty) that seem to exist beyond sensory experience.
    

In short, empiricism says: "We only know what we can experience."
