David Hume was a Scottish philosopher, best known for his work on empiricism and skepticism. Here’s the quick rundown:

1. **Empiricism**: Hume believed that all knowledge comes from sensory experience. We don’t have innate ideas—everything we know is based on what we see, hear, touch, etc.
    
2. **Impressions and Ideas**: He distinguished between "impressions" (strong, vivid sensory experiences) and "ideas" (fainter copies of impressions in our minds).
    
3. **Cause and Effect**: Hume argued that we can never directly observe causality, only the constant conjunction of events. We assume cause and effect based on habit, not rational proof.
    
4. **Problem of Induction**: He famously pointed out that we can’t justify the reasoning behind inductive inferences (like predicting the future based on past events). Just because something has happened repeatedly doesn’t guarantee it will continue.
    
5. **Skepticism about Religion**: Hume was critical of religious arguments, particularly miracles. He argued that the evidence for miracles was weak, and any extraordinary claim should be met with skepticism.
    
6. **Ethics and Morality**: He believed morality is not based on reason, but on sentiment—our feelings and emotions determine what is right or wrong.
    

Hume’s ideas challenged the traditional views of reason, faith, and knowledge, influencing modern philosophy and psychology.
